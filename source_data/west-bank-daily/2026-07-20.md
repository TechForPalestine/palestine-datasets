The reporting period for this section is 14 to 20 July unless otherwise specified.

Across the West Bank, including East Jerusalem, Israeli forces' operations, demolitions, settlement expansion, settler violence and movement restrictions continued to drive displacement, heighten protection risks, and undermine Palestinians' access to housing, livelihoods and essential services.

During the third week of July, Shelter Cluster partners reached approximately 1,180 people (228 households) through about 224 interventions, primarily minor-to-moderate shelter repairs, alongside essential non-food items, protection kits and, where feasible, cash assistance. Response prioritized communities affected by displacement, demolitions and settler violence, particularly in Nablus, Ramallah and Hebron governorates. Meanwhile, the prolonged displacement of more than 33,000 Palestine refugees from Tulkarm and Nur Shams refugee camps continued to generate significant shelter needs, but funding shortfalls continue to limit partners' capacity to scale up assistance.

Since the beginning of 2026, Child Protection partners have provided case management and referrals to specialized services for 758 children and caregivers affected by settler violence, Israeli forces' operations, movement restrictions, displacement and deteriorating socioeconomic conditions. Partners also provided cash-for-protection, in-kind assistance and clothing kits to 1,331 children and 211 caregivers.

Health Cluster partners continued to deliver life-saving services through a network of 870 health service points, including 230 mobile clinic locations. During the third week of July, mobile teams reached 232 of the 249 communities requiring these services (approximately 205,800 people), while about 10 Barrier-isolated communities in the Seam Zone remained inaccessible. Ongoing military operations, movement restrictions, settler violence, shortages of essential medicines and funding shortfalls continued to constrain humanitarian health operations.

Casualties

Between 14 and 20 July, Israeli forces shot and killed two Palestinian men and injured two others during a settler attack in Deir Jarir village, east of Ramallah, after dozens of settlers, some armed and accompanied by Israeli forces, raided the village while attempting to seize sheep. Separately, on 18 July, a 17-year-old Palestinian boy succumbed to wounds sustained six days earlier after Israeli settlers, accompanied by Israeli forces, attacked a Palestinian home in Al Mughayyir village, also in Ramallah governorate.

These incidents reflect a broader rise in fatalities linked to settler attacks. As of 20 July, 18 Palestinians had been killed in such incidents in 2026, including 12 by Israeli settlers, three by Israeli forces, and three where it remains disputed whether Israeli settlers or Israeli forces were responsible. This already exceeds the 17 fatalities recorded in all of 2025. More than half of those killed in settler attacks this year (10 of 18) were in Ramallah governorate.

Overall, during the reporting period, about 36 Palestinians, including two children and eight women, were injured by Israeli forces or settlers. More than 90 per cent were injured in the context of settler attacks, while the remainder were injured mainly during Israeli forces' raids and other operations. Nearly 30 Palestinians were injured by Israeli settlers, including nine during a single attack targeting Palestinian herders, among them five women, in Isfey al Fauqa community in Firing Zone 918, Masafer Yatta, south of Hebron governorate, where armed settlers assaulted residents while attempting to seize livestock.

Since the beginning of 2026, about 880 Palestinians have been injured in the context of settler attacks, including 720 by Israeli settlers and the remainder by Israeli forces, accounting for 56 per cent of all Palestinian injuries in the West Bank. The number of injuries by Israeli settlers linked to settler attacks has risen sharply, increasing from an average of one injury every three days in 2020 to two injuries per day in 2025 and more than three per day so far in 2026.

Settler Attacks and Property Damage

During the reporting period, at least 50 settler attacks that resulted in casualties, property damage, or both were documented, of which nearly 60 per cent were in Ramallah and Nablus governorates. Since the beginning of 2026, more than 1,330 such incidents have been recorded across 250 Palestinian communities, averaging more than six incidents per day.

During the reporting period, five settler attacks targeting Palestinian homes and community infrastructure were documented in Burqa (Ramallah), Khalet al Luza (Bethlehem) and At Tuwani in Masafer Yatta (Hebron). The predominantly night-time attacks involved arson, vandalism, stone throwing and attempted break-ins, damaging at least five homes, a mosque, a community dairy factory and other civilian property, including water infrastructure, a livelihood vehicle and a residential solar water heating system. At least 570 Palestinians were directly affected, including about 560 residents of At Tuwani, among them 58 farmers who rely on the village dairy factory for their livelihoods. Beyond the physical damage, the attacks disrupted livelihoods and community services and heightened protection concerns, particularly following repeated night-time raids on residential areas.

Similar protection concerns were reported in Jalud village, south of Nablus governorate, where Israeli settlers have continued to occupy an under-construction Palestinian house since 29 June. As of the end of the reporting period, the takeover had effectively isolated three neighbouring households comprising nine people from the village centre and essential services. Since early July, settlers have repeatedly vandalized nearby homes and infrastructure, including water pipelines, water tanks and electricity cables, while preventing residents from carrying out repairs. As of the end of the reporting period, the affected households had been without electricity for more than four days after coordination through the Palestinian District Coordination Liaison (DCL) to facilitate repairs was unsuccessful. The families remain increasingly isolated, with limited access to water, electricity, and essential services.
